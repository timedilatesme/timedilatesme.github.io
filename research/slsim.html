SLSim

An open-source package that simulates populations of strong lenses as observatories such as the Rubin Observatory's LSST, Euclid and Roman will see them, including lenses that change over time. I'm one of its lead developers.

1 · A population

Lenses come in populations

Forecasts like the one for lensing tomography need the whole population of lenses a survey will find, not one lens. SLSim builds it: it draws deflector and source galaxies from population models through a cosmological volume, and keeps the configurations that produce strong lensing.

2 · The rare alignments

A few line up

Seen from the observer, only a small fraction of galaxies sit close enough behind a massive foreground galaxy to be strongly lensed. These sight lines are the lenses.

Sources can be static, like galaxies, or variable, like lensed quasars and supernovae.

3 · Across observatories

One population, many telescopes

Each system is then simulated as a given observatory would record it: its pixels, its resolution, its noise and its cadence. From the ground, the Rubin Observatory's LSST is limited by the blurring of the atmosphere; from space, Euclid and Roman see much sharper.

4 · Lenses that change

Time delays

Some lensed sources vary: quasars flicker, and supernovae brighten and fade. Every image shows the same event, but the light of each image takes a different path, so the images change one after another.

These time delays, days to months for a galaxy-scale lens, depend on cosmological distances and so on H0.

In short

Simulating the lenses surveys will find

New surveys will find very large numbers of strong lenses. To plan how to find and use them, we need realistic simulated samples: the right mix of deflectors and sources, at the right distances, imaged the way each telescope would image them.

SLSim draws deflector and source galaxies from population models through a cosmological volume and keeps the configurations that produce strong lensing. It then simulates each system as a given observatory would record it. The Rubin Observatory's LSST, Euclid and Roman are supported out of the box, and other observatories can be added. Sources can be static galaxies or variable ones: lensed quasars and supernovae, with their time delays.

The simulated samples are used to train and test lens searches and analysis pipelines, and to forecast what each survey's sample can measure. SLSim grew out of the LSST Strong Lensing and Dark Energy Science Collaborations. It is open source, and contributions are welcome.
